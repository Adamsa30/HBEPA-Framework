# HBEPA Statutory Innovation Brief: Proposed FLSA Amendment
## Section Title: Presence-Based Compensability for Workforce Preservation

### 1. Statutory Amendment Draft Wording
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled, that Section 3 of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 (29 U.S.C. § 203) is amended by adding at the end the following new subsection:

> **"(z) Workforce Preservation Sessions Under Approved Accommodation.—**
> 
> **(1) IN GENERAL.—** For the purposes of subsection (g) ['Employ' defined], an employee who is logged into an authorized, encrypted remote session under an approved Home-Based Employment Preservation Accommodation (HBEPA) program shall be deemed to be engaged in compensable tracking time during periods of verified network session persistence.
> 
> **(2) NONPRODUC_EXCLUSION.—** Hours logged under paragraph (1) shall be tracked exclusively under the statutory designation *'Approved Employment Preservation Accommodation Time'*. Such sessions are explicitly decoupled from standard active manufacturing, customer-service fulfillment, or production quotas. 
> 
> **(3) SAFE HARBOR RULES.—** No employer utilizing a certified, zero-trust presence verification architecture that adheres to strict privacy-by-design standards (prohibiting geographical telemetry and video surveillance monitoring) shall be held in violation of minimum-wage or hours-worked provisions under Section 6 or Section 7 for maintaining an individual's professional connectivity and benefit structures during periods of acute task-related functional impairment."

---

### 2. Legal Analysis & Justification
*   **Decoupling Presence from Output:** Traditional FLSA frameworks rely heavily on the definition of work as a physical task. This amendment provides a legal safe harbor, allowing organizations to maintain individuals on the payroll via network persistence while protecting both parties from production metrics disputes.
*   **Enforcing Supervisory Blindness:** By legalizing a flat binary indicator as proof of active status, the statute explicitly shields the employee's underlying diagnostic history from operational line managers, maintaining alignment with the HIPAA Minimum-Necessary principle.
